# Le rundown porte la lecture de la page, les blocs portent leurs aides de lecture

The Cahier's current prototype renders an interpretive prose paragraph beside every ContentSection, in addition to the page introduction and figure explanations. A page that contains several related figures therefore reads like a report assembled from several mini-essays. The duplication is especially visible in the Mobilité « Accès aux services » ContentUnit: the same access tension is restated in the summary, profiles, essential-services, and distribution blocks.

**Decision (user/PO, 2026-09-03, #549):** the main data-deterministic interpretation moves to one short **Page rundown** on the ContentUnit/page subtitle. It states the page's central tension and its most useful qualification; it is not a concatenation of every block's prose or a list of every figure result. Each ContentSection keeps a local **Marelle** as its question or tension and may keep a compact **Comment lire** helper explaining the figure's denominator, threshold, unit, or honest unavailable state. Full block-level interpretation is exceptional and must earn its space by explaining something the page rundown and figure cannot.

**Considered options:** (1) keep a prose paragraph beside every figure — rejected: repeated conclusions make the page feel like a written report and bury the evidence; (2) move every existing paragraph into the subtitle — rejected: the subtitle would become a wall of text and would still enumerate blocks; (3) remove all explanatory text — rejected: figures need local orientation, particularly for thresholds, denominators, and incomplete data; (4) use only a generic static subtitle — rejected: the page reading must remain data-deterministic and territory-specific.

**Consequences:** the semantic model needs a page/ContentUnit-level `rundown` separate from section `Lecture` content. The renderer places the rundown in the page subtitle, renders Marelles as block headings, and renders Comment lire helpers locally; it does not invent or assemble prose. The Mobilité prose logic is condensed and moved through #549, beginning with the Accès aux services page. Tests must assert the page-level content seam, non-duplication, both Cahier presentations, and honest complete/incomplete/absent states. This is a content-architecture decision rather than a figure change: #545's evidence and geometry remain unchanged.
